Propaganda strategy

Our course describes propaganda as communication meant "to influence minds". This is our plan to change one belief held by heads of talent at homegrown consulting firms. Deliverable 2 of 4.

The engagement ends. The evidence stays.

Audience
Heads of talent at homegrown Indian consulting firms of 40 to 400 people, and their managing partners.
Expected response
Take the 2-minute walkthrough and book a call, then propose a 12-week paid pilot to the managing partner.
Belief today
"Nobody remembers a consultant's whole year, and that is how consulting works."
Belief we want
"The record breaks up with every engagement team, and we can fix that."

Audience

We write for the head of talent at a homegrown Indian consulting firm of 40 to 400 people. The head of talent feels the problem first and would run a pilot, but cannot buy alone. The managing partner signs, and any practice partner can block it. Our illustrative personas are Kavya, head of talent at a 150-person firm, and Vikram, its managing partner.

Belief

Today the head of talent hears that losing a consultant's year is just how consulting works, or that it is politics. That was true when partners saw everyone's work. Now people rotate across engagements, and the record breaks up with each team. We want heads of talent to believe that this can be fixed, and that fixing it gives partners more to judge with. One late rating is weak evidence: in two samples of over 2,000 managers each, the rater's own habits explained more of a rating than the manager's performance did (Scullen, Mount and Goff, 2000).

Central message

The line starts from a moment every head of talent knows. Engagements end every few weeks, and what the team saw leaves with them. The cause is how work rotates, so the line blames nobody and partners can hear it without getting defensive. Most people see a post or a standee once, so it also has to work in one reading.

Tactics and channels

We give the head of talent a name for the problem to use with partners, and every piece carries the same line. Readers can check the product themselves in the walkthrough, with no form. The channels are free and use no ad pixels or bought lists: the Sphyr LinkedIn page, whose posts the team reshares, warm introductions, HR community meets we attend as guests, articles in HR media and our website. The one advertisement is a planned standee at an HR community event, up in the same opening weeks as the first of three LinkedIn posts, which go out two weeks apart.

Why they fit

Heads of talent are short of time and wary of vendors, so the proof has to be checkable: the price sits on the home page and the walkthrough asks for nothing. Heads of talent trust peers more than vendors, so we go through introductions and HR community meets. A firm that wants consultants to trust a fuller record will not trust a vendor that follows people around the web.

Link to positioning

Our positioning calls Sphyr 360 feedback that takes two minutes every two weeks, from the people each consultant works with, so partners decide on the whole year. Moving from engagement to engagement is the cause we name. Two minutes a fortnight answers the fear of extra admin, and partners deciding answers the partner's veto.

Expected response

The first sign is a head of talent who tells partners the record breaks up with the team. A visit to the 2-minute walkthrough and a 20-minute call follow, then a pilot proposal: 12 weeks, up to 40 people, ₹25,000 plus GST. Asked who else has tried it, we show the four measures agreed before every pilot and the founder's 2018 paper pilot, where the cards for an average circle of 14 people took a little over two minutes (02:09).
