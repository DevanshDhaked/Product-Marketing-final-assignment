Propaganda strategy

In our course, propaganda means communication that sets out to influence minds, and advertising is the route where money is spent. This strategy is about changing one belief held by heads of talent and managing partners at homegrown consulting firms, so that by the time they see Sphyr, the problem it solves already feels real to them and fixable.

1The audience we want to influence

We are talking to homegrown Indian management and strategy consulting firms led by their partners, with about 40 to 400 professionals. Four groups inside each firm matter to us, and each needs something different.

The head of talent, our champion
The head of talent feels the problem first, in project-end forms that never arrive and in exit interviews. This is the person who finds Sphyr and runs the pilot, then takes the case to the partners. They need words for the problem that partners will accept, and a small way to test the fix.
The managing partner, who signs
The managing partner chairs the meeting where partners argue about promotions, and needs to hear that partners keep the decision and that the test is small and measured.
Practice partners and engagement managers, who can veto
They fear losing discretion and gaining admin. They need to see that Sphyr adds to what they judge with and costs them two minutes a fortnight.
Consultants, the people rated
Their trust decides whether anyone fills in a card during a 60-hour client week. They need a two-minute task and a record of their own that they can read.

The person we planned around

Kavya, Vikram and Meera are illustrative composites built from research on the segment. None of them is a real person, and none of their words come from an interview.

Kavya is head of talent at a 150-person firm with eleven partners across three offices. Every year a partner asks Kavya for "the file" on a consultant the partner barely staffed, and Kavya sends two thin forms and a forwarded client email. A senior consultant who left last year told Kavya that the partner who did the rating had seen three weeks of the consultant's work. This year Meera, a senior consultant, has worked on four engagements under three engagement managers, and a client CFO praised Meera's model to the partner. Kavya can see the same year happening again. Vikram, the managing partner, signs. Vikram worries about good juniors leaving, and about two partners describing the same person in opposite terms because each saw a different engagement.

Who we are not trying to influence, and why

Firms under about 40 people
Partners there still see everyone's work, so the belief we want to change is still mostly true for them.
Global firms, including MBB and the Big 4
They run global review systems and buy through global procurement. A 12-week pilot proposed by a head of talent does not fit how they buy.
IT services and technology consulting
Another team in the course works on that domain, and the way people are staffed there is different.
Staffing firms that place people at one client for years
The client's own manager sees the work every day, so the record does not break up when an engagement ends.
Buyers who want to monitor activity
Sphyr holds only what colleagues choose to say on a card. Chasing these buyers would cost us the consultants' trust.

2The belief we want to encourage

AspectTodayAfter the campaign
Belief"Losing track of a consultant's year is just how consulting works." Or: "It's politics.""The record breaks up with every engagement team, and we can fix that. Fixing it gives partners more to judge with."

Why the current belief holds

When the firm was small, a few partners worked next to everyone, so the belief was true then. Growth took that away slowly, so there was never one moment that felt like a failure. Partners also trust their own judgement, and any message that sounds like "your partners are biased" gets rejected in the first meeting.

How we move it

We name a cause that every partner will admit to: people rotate across engagements, and memory fades. By the time partners sit down to decide, the engagement manager who saw someone's best work is on another engagement or has left the firm. Seen that way, the problem comes from how consulting work is organised. A partner can agree with it without admitting that anyone did anything wrong, and a head of talent can raise it without accusing the partners.

3The central message and the reasoning behind it

The engagement ends. The evidence stays.

In full: Sphyr collects the view of the people who saw a consultant's work, about two minutes every two weeks, while the engagement team is still together, so partners can decide on the whole year.

Why this line

It starts from a moment the head of talent already knows: engagements end every few weeks, and what the team saw goes with them. Because an engagement ends on its own schedule, the line blames nobody, so partners and consultants can both hear it without bristling. For a partner, more evidence on the table makes a decision easier to defend. Most people will see a post or a standee once, so we also needed a line that works in one reading and can stay the same everywhere.

The reasons to believe, each with its source

Ratings say a lot about the rater
In two data sets of managers (2,350 and 2,142 people, each rated by seven raters), idiosyncratic rater effects explained 62% and 53% of the variance in ratings. The person's actual performance explained 21% and 25% (Scullen, Mount and Goff, 2000) [1].
People doubt their reviews
Gallup reports that 29% of employees strongly agree their reviews are fair and 26% that they are accurate. The figure is global, and the sample size is not stated [2].
Consulting has already moved
Deloitte moved to short "snapshots" completed "at the end of a project, phase, or at least quarterly" [3]. Deloitte has no connection with Sphyr.
The method takes about two minutes
In a 2018 paper-and-pen pilot run by Sphyr's founder in four companies, a card took a little over two minutes (02:09) for an average circle of 14 people [4]. It tested the method on paper, with self-reported results; today's software came later.

4Tactics and channels

Tactics

Give the problem a name
The core line gives the head of talent words to use with a partner. The first LinkedIn post describes the moment a partner asks for "the file" and asks readers what they send.
Choose the cause with care
We talk about rotation and fading memory. No piece describes partners as unfair or biased.
Repeat one idea
Every piece we publish, from the website to the ad, carries the same line and the same picture: engagements ending while one record runs through them.
Show the workings
The site explains how cards and pooling work and what managers can see. It also lists what Sphyr does not do, next to the price and the pilot terms, so the reader has less to take on trust.
Build proof in public
Every pilot is measured on four things agreed before it starts: how many people take part in each cycle, how many different people give feedback to each consultant, whether partners found something new in the records, and whether consultants found their own record useful. Results stay with the firm unless it agrees to share them.
Let the reader check it
In the walkthrough a head of talent or a partner clicks through a card and a record in two minutes, with no form to fill in, and judges the product on what they saw.

Channels

All of them are free except the one advertisement. They follow two rules from our channel plan: people choose to hear from us, and we track nobody.

Sphyr's LinkedIn company page
It carries the three posts, and the team reshares them from their own profiles. Heads of talent and partners read LinkedIn for work, and a post a peer reshares reaches them through someone they know.
Warm introductions
One personal note per firm, sent through our own and our alumni's networks to a head of talent the sender knows, asking permission to share the walkthrough.
National HRD Network chapters
We attend chapter meets as participants and offer a talk on calibration when people rotate, if a chapter wants one. This is where heads of talent compare notes with peers.
The Institute of Management Consultants of India
Consultants and some partners meet here. We would offer a talk or a written piece on keeping track of work across engagements.
Contributed articles
HR media that accept pieces from practitioners bring readers we could not reach alone, under an editor's standards.
A monthly note
By sign-up only, with one-click unsubscribe. It keeps the relationship going after a first visit without chasing anyone.
Our own website
Every other channel points here, to the walkthrough, the method, the price and the pilot terms.
The advertisement
A standee with matching handout cards, planned for an HR community event; nothing is booked. It is our one paid placement, because in our course advertising is the route where money is involved. Its QR code opens a plain web address, and nobody is tracked.

What we never do: retargeting or tracking pixels, bought or scraped contact lists, automated LinkedIn messages, cold mass email, planted comments, or anything that suggests a community, our faculty or our university endorses Sphyr.

5Why these tactics fit this audience and this objective

The objective is a change of belief, and the first person whose belief we need is a head of talent who will then have to persuade the partners, so each choice above starts from what that person needs.

Heads of talent are sceptical of vendors and short of time, so the evidence has to be something they can check themselves: the walkthrough asks for no form, and the sources and the price are on the page. They hear about new ideas from peers, so the channels are peer rooms and introductions, and the posts are written to be forwarded. The partners they have to persuade will reject anything that sounds like a complaint about them, which is why the cause we name is rotation and memory. Consultants decide whether the record exists at all, so nothing we publish talks about watching or measuring them, and the site says plainly what managers can see.

The zero-budget rule and the no-tracking rule suit the same audience. A firm that wants its own people to trust a fuller record will not trust a vendor that follows them around the web. Keeping the paid part to one standee, in a room the head of talent chose to walk into, is consistent with what we ask firms to believe about Sphyr.

6How the strategy connects to positioning

Our positioning has a vision line and a product line.

Vision
"Careers in consulting should be earned in front of the people who saw the work, not decided by whoever remembers it."
Product
"For homegrown consulting firms where people move from engagement to engagement, Sphyr is 360 feedback that takes two minutes every two weeks, from the people each consultant works with. Unlike a year-end review written from memory or one manager's end-of-project form, it keeps the record when the team breaks up, so partners decide on the whole year."

Each part has a job in this strategy. "People move from engagement to engagement" is the cause we name when we shift the belief. "Two minutes every two weeks" answers the fear of admin, and "partners decide on the whole year" answers the partner's veto. The vision line gives a consultant a reason to want this, and the core line compresses all of it into seven words.

Every piece follows the same order: the moment the engagement ends, the two-minute mechanism, what partners see, the vision, then one next step. The website uses all five. The posts stop earlier and end on a question or the walkthrough. The ad keeps the first and third steps and ends on the walkthrough.

7The action and response we expect

A head of talent who describes the problem to a partner as a record that breaks up with the team has already changed their mind, before clicking anything. After that we expect these steps, in order.

  1. The head of talent recognises the problem and says so: a comment on the first post, a saved post, a post forwarded to a partner.
  2. They take the 2-minute walkthrough.
  3. They book a 20-minute call with our team.
  4. They take a pilot proposal to the managing partner: 12 weeks, six fortnightly cycles, up to 40 people, for ₹25,000 plus GST, credited against the first annual bill if the firm signs. If it does not sign, the firm gets its data as a CSV file and the rest is deleted.
  5. After the pilot, the firm decides on its own scorecard. The price is ₹149 per employee per month, billed yearly, plus GST, for any firm size.

We will know it is working without tracking anyone. We count walkthrough visits as one weekly total for the whole site, we ask "where did you hear about us?" on every call, and we count pilots. Our estimate to test is three to five paid pilots by week 8 of the sequence below.

8The campaign sequence over the selling season

We count weeks from the first post and work back from week 22, when partners calibrate. A 12-week pilot has to start by week 8 so that it ends with time left for partners to read the records.

9Objections we expect, and our answers

A partner: "So now HR rates my team?"
Partners still decide. They see the view of the people who worked with each consultant on every engagement, and they fill in the same two-minute cards as everyone else.
A head of talent: "My consultants won't fill in another form in a 60-hour week."
A card takes about two minutes, every two weeks, for the people in their circle. In the 2018 paper pilot it took 02:09 for a circle of 14. Each consultant also gets to read their own record.
A consultant: "This is HR watching us."
Sphyr holds only what colleagues choose to say on a card. Ratings are pooled and comments are anonymized to the person rated. A manager who approves a request to edit feedback can see who gave it, and the site says so.
A partner: "Will my clients be asked to rate my people?"
Only when the consultant invites a client by email, with the partner's agreement. The client relationship stays with the partner.
"We already have reviews in our HR system."
Sphyr runs next to it, and people are imported from a spreadsheet. It adds what the HR system does not keep: a view from everyone a consultant worked with, every two weeks, across engagements.
"Who else has tried this?"
We measure every pilot on the same four things, agreed with the firm before it starts, and the firm keeps its results unless it agrees to share them. The method itself was tried on paper in 2018, and the walkthrough lets you check the product in two minutes.
The managing partner: "What if it shows us nothing?"
The pilot costs ₹25,000 plus GST, which is what it costs us to run. If the firm does not sign, it gets its data as a CSV file and the rest is deleted.

10Our limits

Propaganda can mislead, and a product that asks firms to trust a fuller record cannot afford to. We do not invent customers or reviews, and every statistic has a named source. Every story is labelled as an illustration. The 2018 pilot is always described as a paper-and-pen trial that the founder ran, with self-reported results. We never call the feedback anonymous without saying what managers can see. We do not push a decision with fear of losing people, or with deadlines and limited places that are not real. We name National HRD Network chapters and IMCI only as places we take part in; we have no partnership with either, and neither endorses Sphyr.
