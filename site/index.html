The problem

At 40 people you saw everyone's work.
At 200, you see whoever reaches you.

Nobody did anything wrong. The company just grew. Now each promotion leans on one manager's view of one person, and that view goes to whoever is most visible.

The loudest voice gets the raise

The person who presents the work gets the credit. The person who did the work gets thanked.

Last month counts more than the whole year

Annual reviews depend on what one person remembers. That's usually the last few weeks.

The quiet contributor quietly leaves

The engineer who unblocked three teams never shows up in a review. One day she gets a better offer.

One view vs. everyone's view

One rater is a guess. Fourteen, every fortnight, is evidence.

Today: one manager's memory

One view, once a year. In a study of two large datasets of multi-rater ratings, more than half of the variance in a rating came from the person giving it, not the person being rated (62% and 53%; Scullen, Mount & Goff, Journal of Applied Psychology, 2000).

Sphyr: everyone who worked with them

Manager, peers, juniors, other leads, even clients and vendors: their Sphere. Each gives two minutes a fortnight. Pooled and anonymized, one person's quirks get averaged out.

Meera's story · illustrative, not a customer case

Why a Head of People goes looking

Trigger

Meera runs People at a growing IT services company. A strong engineer resigns weeks after being passed over. In the exit interview she says: "Nobody saw what I did."

Search

The founder still signs off every promotion but no longer works next to most people. Meera needs evidence she can bring him, not another opinion.

Evaluate

She finds the price published, the anonymity rules spelled out, and a walkthrough she can try without talking to sales. She sends it to the founder.

Act

They run a six-week paid pilot on one team. By the third cycle the founder can see work he couldn't see before.

The fortnight loop

Two minutes, every two weeks

Cards arrive

Each person gets a card for everyone in their Sphere. Rating, tags, optional note. About five clicks each.

Ratings pool

Responses are anonymized and pooled. Nothing shows until enough reviewers have answered.

Notes release in batches

Written notes are delayed and released together, so timing can't reveal who wrote them.

Evidence builds

By appraisal time, managers have months of pooled ratings across seven parameters. They still make the call.
