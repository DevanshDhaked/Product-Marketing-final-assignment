The advertisement

A roll-up standee and a matching handout card, planned for a paid sponsor spot at an HR community event.

Why this ad

Medium
A roll-up standee on a paid sponsor spot at an HR community event, with A6 cards on a stand beside it for people to take. In our class notes, advertising is the push route where "money is involved", so the placement is a paid one. The same poster put up for free would count as word of mouth.
Audience
Heads of talent at partner-led consulting firms of about 40 to 400 people, and the partners who sometimes come with them. The room will hold HR people from every industry, so the copy uses consulting words like engagement and calibrate. The right people stop, and everyone else can walk on.
Message
The core line, with one supporting line on how it works.
Benefit
Partners have a year of views from the people who saw each consultant's work.
Visual
The ribbon from the website. Four project boxes fade one after another while a single line (the consultant's record) runs through all of them and stays clear at the end. There are no faces or scores on it.
Call to action
Take the 2-minute walkthrough. The QR code and the printed address open the same page, and it asks for nothing.
Journey stage
The trigger, when a head of talent first puts a name to the problem. It runs in the same weeks as the first LinkedIn post.
Placement
Planned for an HR community event of the kind run by national HR network chapters. Paying for a spot does not mean the organisers endorse Sphyr.
Why it tracks nobody
Nobody's badge is scanned and nobody fills in a form. The QR code holds the plain page address with no tracking parameters, so a scan tells us nothing about who made it. People who are curious can look, and everyone else can walk past.
