The advertisement

A roll-up standee and a matching handout card, planned for a paid sponsor spot at an HR community event.

Why this ad

Medium
A roll-up standee on a paid sponsor spot, with A6 cards on a stand beside it for people to take. It is planned for an HR community event of the kind national HR network chapters run, and paying for the spot does not mean the organisers endorse Sphyr. We pay for the spot because the course treats advertising as the route where money is spent.
Audience
Heads of talent at Indian consulting firms of 40 to 400 people, and the partners who come with them. The room holds HR people from every industry, so the headline uses a moment only a consulting firm has, and everyone else can walk on.
Why this headline
Most people walking past read only the headline, so the headline is the Attention: it has to stop them. It names a week every head of talent at a consulting firm knows: calibration is close and the project feedback has not come in, so it needs no second line to explain it. The Sphyr name sits above it, and the line under it says what Sphyr is and who it is for, then asks for a call.
Journey stage
The trigger, when a head of talent first puts a name to the problem. It runs in the same weeks as the first LinkedIn post.
Why it tracks nobody
Nobody's badge is scanned and nobody fills in a form. The QR code and the printed address open the same plain page with no tracking parameters, so a scan tells us nothing about who made it.
