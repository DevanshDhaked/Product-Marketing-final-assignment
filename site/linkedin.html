SphyrPromote on merit, not politics. · Week 1, Tuesday
She unblocked three teams last quarter. Her name came up in zero promotion discussions. A story most Heads of People at growing companies will recognise. (Illustrative, but you've probably lived it.) Priya is a backend engineer at a 200-person company. When the payments team got stuck, she fixed the API. When a new joiner was lost, she paired with him for a week. When the release slipped, she stayed late. None of it happened in front of her manager. At appraisal time, her manager did what busy managers do: remembered the last few weeks, and the people who presented the work. Priya got "meets expectations". The person who demoed her fix got promoted. Six weeks later she resigned. Exit interview: "Nobody saw what I did." Nobody here is a villain. The manager was stretched. The founder used to know everyone, until the company grew past what any one person can see. That's the real problem. Growth outpaces sight, but promotions still run on one person's view. SphyrPromote on merit, not politics. · Week 3, Tuesday
In 1906, 787 people at a country fair guessed the weight of an ox. Most weren't experts. Individually, many were way off. Francis Galton took the median of all 787 guesses: 1,207 lb. The ox weighed 1,198 lb. Less than 1% out. (Galton, "Vox Populi", Nature, 1906.) Now look at how most companies decide promotions: → One judge (the manager) → One view → Once a year And research says a single judge tells you a lot about the judge. Across two large datasets, more than half the variance in performance ratings came from the person giving the rating, not the person being rated. (Scullen, Mount & Goff, Journal of Applied Psychology, 2000.) Your managers aren't bad judges. Each is just one judge. The fix isn't finding better managers. It's adding more views: peers, juniors, other leads, even clients. Keep each one short, anonymous and frequent. Pool them over months, and one person's blind spots average out. Promote on merit, not politics. Built from everyone who actually worked with them, not one manager's memory. See what pooled feedback looks like. Two-minute walkthrough, no sign-up.
