## 1. What Sphyr is

Sphyr is a continuous 360° feedback tool for performance reviews. Every fortnight, each employee gets two-minute anonymous ratings from the people they work with. Promotions are decided on pooled feedback, not on one manager's memory. It runs alongside the company's existing HR system and does not replace it.

Source: Sphyr Overview Whitepaper v0.3 (2018, Ravi Warrier) and Sphyr.io Product Documentation.

## 3. Segmentation

**Working target (from the project memory file and the positioning work):**
- Founders of growing companies of 50–300 people who approve promotions but can't see who did what.
- The pain sits in the band where the founder has lost line of sight but still signs off on promotions.

**Candidate segment work (Sep 2026):**
- Mid-size IT services and digital engineering firms in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Delhi NCR and Chennai, with roughly ₹50–400 crore annual revenue.
- Global Capability Centres and product/SaaS companies were flagged as the most interesting adjacent segments.
- Poor-fit categories were also mapped.

**Method used:** Kotler's four bases (drivers vs. descriptors), then his five effectiveness tests (measurable, substantial, accessible, differentiable, actionable). Each segment gets a verdict such as TARGET — primary, SECONDARY, AWARENESS ONLY or DO NOT TARGET.

**(open)** The segment size conflicts across our work. The deck persona uses 200–1,000 employees, while the positioning statement uses 50–300. The segmentation uses a revenue band, but the pain is driven by headcount. Decide one band and state revenue for segment definition and headcount for messaging.

## 6. Positioning and messaging

### Positioning statement (current reference version)
> For founders of 50–300-person companies who sign off on promotions but can't see who did what, Sphyr is a 360° feedback layer alongside their HR system that lets them promote on merit, not politics, because it pools two-minute anonymized ratings every fortnight from everyone an employee works with.

**Shorter options we produced:**
- 41 words: keeps every part of the statement.
- 36 words: replaces "can't see who did what" with "blind". Shorter but less concrete.
- 28 words (our pick in the shortlist): "For founders of 50–300-person companies who can't see who did what, Sphyr pools fortnightly anonymous ratings from everyone around each employee, so promotions run on merit, not politics."
- 21 words and a 5-word tagline ("Promote on merit, not politics.") were also drafted.

**Rules we settled on:**
- Keep "two-minute", "anonymous" and "everyone". These make Sphyr hard to copy.
- Use "50–300" internally. Use "growing companies" on the website and in ads.
- Use headcount in the statement. Keep the revenue band for segment definition.
- Don't use "approve promotions blind". Abhinav objected that it blames the founder. Reframe the pain as company growth outpacing founder visibility.
- Alternate phrase if "politics" draws objections: "merit, not memory".

### Vision statement
Five themes were drafted: Earned, not politicked (recommended as most ownable); Everyone has a voice; Growth, not judgement; Employee delight; and one more. We produced several shorter versions of the chosen vision (33 words down to 26, then five further variants with word counts and trade-offs). Bheema had a preference for one earlier variant.

### Messaging hooks and copy
- Core central line: "Built from everyone who actually worked with them, not one manager's memory. That's Sphyr."
- Drafted: long-form blog or LinkedIn opener, Instagram caption with hashtags, four-slide image card sequence, and a skeptic-facing version.
- Review-site channels: G2, Capterra (India URL noted), TrustRadius.

### Positioning method
We used the HubSpot positioning-statement framework (target, need, category, differentiating benefit, reason to believe) and Dunford's components, plus the playbook's evidence labels.

## 7. Packaging (Team 03)

**Promise:** for founders of growing companies that have outgrown their line of sight, Sphyr pools fortnightly anonymous ratings from everyone around each employee, so promotions run on merit, not politics.

**Customer-facing elements:**
- **Website:** problem-led home, how it works, trust page (anonymity, data handling, refund terms), guides on appraisals.
- **Good demo:** a two-minute interactive walkthrough, no sign-up, on labelled sample data.
- **Guided setup:** welcome kit, kickoff call, employee import and permissions, role-based video training, first-cycle support, handover.
- **Integration:** runs alongside the current HR system. CSV/XLSX import today; direct HRMS connectors later.

**AIDA alignment:**
- **Awareness:** everything public, including price and data handling.
- **Interest/decision:** try the walkthrough first. Annual plan with a full refund if the first six fortnightly cycles (about 12 weeks) show nothing new. Renewal terms stated upfront, with a reminder 30 days before.
- **Action:** one-page online order that carries details over from the demo request. Incentive: one month's credit at first renewal.

**Founder's invite copy and FAQ** are drafted (e.g. "Can my manager see who wrote what? No.").

**(open)** Packaging says "public pricing by company size". This conflicts with the single flat rate below. Pick one.

## 8. Pricing

**Current agreed model (Nov 2026):**
- One flat rate of ₹149 per employee per month, billed annually, plus GST.
- No tiers, no free plan, and no volume discounts.
- Clients and vendors who give feedback are always free.
- Entry: ₹15,000 paid six-week pilot, credited against the first bill.
- First 25 customers get a two-year price lock in exchange for a case study. This is a trade for social proof, not a discount.

**Justification (three pillars):**
- **Cost floor:** about ₹30 per employee in cost ÷ 80% target margin ≈ ₹150.
- **Customer value:** about ₹667 per employee per month, from reduced attrition and less review-prep time (based on Gallup attrition-cost data and the whitepaper's time-cost method).
- **Market comparison:** competitors charge about ₹665–1,425 per employee per month. Competitors checked include Keka, Zoho People, Lattice, greytHR, Culture Amp, Synergita, Peoplebox and Leapsome.

**Break-even:** about 67 companies of 100 employees.

**Why we rejected tiered pricing:** the faculty objected to cliff jumps, such as the rate going from ₹30 to ₹100 per employee when one new employee joins.

**Documents:** a two-page pricing PDF and a condensed one-page version (Inter font, accent #0e7c66).

**Assumptions to re-check:** FX ₹95/USD, support costs and infra costs. A friend's pricing draft was reviewed. It had the same tiered-pricing issue, a free plan, volume discounts, and an unverified Keka pricing claim.

**(open)** Keka's pricing claim was flagged as unverified. Confirm before it appears in anything external.
