## Pricing Strategy: 70/100
Graded document: `tasks/saas/Sphyr_Pricing_OnePager.pdf` (course folder).

You have presented a clear and focused pricing proposal. Your decision to use a single, flat per-employee price is a legitimate strategic choice. The proposal is easy to understand, and you have considered the relationship between serving costs, customer value and competitor pricing.

There are, however, some important gaps in your reasoning.

Value calculation: You estimate ₹667 in monthly value per employee, comprising ₹500 from reduced employee attrition and ₹167 from reduced review preparation. The assumptions behind these figures need to be demonstrated. In particular, explain how preventing one resignation per year translates into ₹500 of value for every employee every month.

Competitive comparison: Your competitor price range is useful, but you need to demonstrate that you are comparing similar products and features. Identify the specific plans and explain the basis of comparison.

Pricing rationale: You calculate ₹150 as the price required for an 80% gross margin but recommend ₹149. Explain this decision. Also, if the perceived customer value is ₹667, why have you chosen to capture only ₹149?

Break-even: Your arithmetic is correct, but the ₹8 lakh monthly fixed-cost assumption needs a breakdown. You should also demonstrate that the ₹119 contribution includes all relevant variable costs.

Pilot pricing: The ₹15,000 pilot is an interesting proposal. However, explain how you arrived at this amount, why six weeks is appropriate and what happens if the customer does not convert.

Commercial validation: The Van Westendorp survey is a good choice. Explain how you will use the results to make an actual pricing decision rather than simply collect customer opinions.

Overall, your proposal demonstrates a good understanding of pricing models and their commercial implications. Your main weakness is that some of the most important numbers, particularly customer value and fixed costs, are presented without sufficient supporting logic.

The key improvement is to demonstrate how you arrived at your assumptions and why those assumptions should be trusted.

## Packaging: 69/100

I think you have made a good attempt at understanding what packaging means for a SaaS product. Your website, interactive demo, guided setup, training, employee communication and support are all relevant to the experience of receiving and starting to use Sphyr.

I particularly liked the interactive demo. It gives a prospective customer an opportunity to experience the product before making a purchase decision. I also appreciate that you considered the employees who will use Sphyr, rather than focusing only on the person buying it.

However, I see two significant gaps in your submission.

1. You have focused more on reducing hesitation than creating excitement.
Much of your proposal is about reassuring customers that the product is safe, easy to use and worth trying. These are important objectives, but they are not the same as creating excitement.
A customer can feel reassured about buying something without being particularly excited about receiving it.
I wanted you to think about what would make the customer look forward to the product. What would they anticipate? What would make them feel that something useful or valuable is about to happen? How would you build that anticipation before the first use?

2. A significant part of your submission is about customer acquisition.
Your discussion of AIDA, refund promises, the order process, renewal incentives and customer acquisition costs belongs to sales and marketing strategy. These are not central to the packaging experience.
The assignment was about what happens between the customer first encountering the product and actually using it. Your submission moves beyond that into how to persuade people to buy and how to acquire customers.

3. The first-use experience needs more thought.
Completing a five-click feedback card may be simple, but what does the user get out of that first interaction?
Does it make the user feel that their contribution matters? Does it help them discover something useful? Does it create a reason to return?
Your proposal explains how the user completes the task, but not sufficiently what makes the experience rewarding.

Overall, you have identified many of the right touchpoints and understand the importance of the onboarding experience. However, you need to be more precise about what each element is intended to achieve. Making a product easy to adopt is only part of the job. Packaging should also make the customer excited about the experience that lies ahead.

### Course notes on customer acquisition (from the user's class notes, to be refined with research)

- Customer Acquisition (feasible, viable, desirable).
- AIDA framework (Awareness, Interest, Decision, Action). Diagram: 4 sequential boxes connected by a line passing through checkpoints to a final circular target.
  - A (Awareness): the customer wants "info" about everything surrounding the product or service.
  - I (Interest): the customer wants "assurance" (guarantee, cashback or refund, a manual, and so on).
  - D (Decision): do: gift coupons for the next purchase; don't: make them fill details manually, or add friction with payment.
- CAC (customer acquisition cost): money spent to convert one customer. Also consider expectations, norms and so on.
