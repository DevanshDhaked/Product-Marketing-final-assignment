# Consulting visual language for Sphyr v3

## Method and limits

- No browser and no screenshots (brief). The firecrawl `branding` format fails without Chrome CDP, and the `query` format failed on every site tried (6 of 6). So I read each home page's HTML and its linked CSS with a small script (`.swarm/tmp/look/css_probe.py`). It counts `@font-face` families, `font-family` use, hex colours, images, videos, SVGs and headings. Raw output: `.swarm/tmp/look/*.jsonl`.
- What the script can see: typefaces, colour tokens, image and video counts, the heading text and how many headings there are. What it cannot see: rendered grid, sizes on screen, motion as felt, photo style. Where I describe those, it is an inference from code signals (for example a `gsap` or `lenis` script means scroll motion) and is marked "inferred".
- McKinsey timed out and BCG returned 403 to my script. After a firecrawl restart, firecrawl markdown read the BCG home page and McKinsey's Featured Insights page (the McKinsey home page still returned 403). Their type and colour come from the agencies' and the type foundry's own pages (primary for the brand, secondary for the site).
- Gallery coverage: Awwwards category pages (business-corporate, finance, law, consulting) gave 74 entries; 25 of them are Site of the Day or Honorable Mention. Siteinspire's Corporate category was read through firecrawl. Godly, Land-book, Lapa Ninja and the rest were not reachable in a form I could parse (Godly search returned only its home page; Lapa Ninja returned 403 in the 2026-11-24 scan in `docs/design/references.md`).

### The shared pattern

1. **The home page is a publication.** Bain's 26 headings are all report titles. Oliver Wyman, Roland Berger, Primus, 1Lattice and Redseer lead with or quickly reach insights. On 5 of 11 pages read (Bain, Oliver Wyman, Roland Berger, Primus, 1Lattice) most headings are research titles, and 3 more (Kearney, Redseer, Technopak) put at least one report among the first headings. Thinking, shown as articles and data, is the product.
2. **Serif display over a plain sans, at the global tier.** A serif face is loaded on 5 of 11 pages read, and CSS selectors show it styles headings on 4 (Bain: hero titles and h2; Oliver Wyman: the home hero title; Roland Berger: section headlines and quotes; Redseer: `.font-display` and the scene headlines). On Praxis, Playfair is loaded but I could not tie it to headings. McKinsey and BCG build their identity on custom faces (Bower, Henderson Serif logotype). The mid-size Indian sites mostly use free geometric sans faces (Montserrat or Poppins on 3 of 6: Technopak, Primus, Praxis).
3. **One brand colour on white or a warm neutral.** Bain (red), McKinsey (deep blue), Kearney (yellow on black and white), Redseer (red on warm grey), Oliver Wyman (blues) each run one hue. Roland Berger is the exception with a multicolour palette.
4. **Data as proof, with numbers in headlines.** Sattva's H1 is a number. Redseer leads with "$10 Bn Outlook by 2031". 1Lattice has a section called "1Lattice in Numbers". McKinsey's identity includes a data visualisation system.
5. **Say what the firm is, in the first line.** Redseer: "a strategy consulting firm built to guide critical decisions". ZS: "A consulting and technology firm improving life". This is the professor's Attention point in visual form.
6. **Proof by third parties, not product.** Praxis shows rankings and awards; Setia Law (section 3) does the same. No consulting site shows a product screen, because they do not sell one.

Why (inference, supported by the McKinsey quote): the reader is a senior executive who judges the firm by the quality of its thinking. "We think the contrast depicts our clarity of thought and our ability to cut through and deliver what really matters" (Peter Dahlstrom, senior partner, in Transform, 2019). Restraint signals seriousness; the content (reports, numbers, rankings) carries the weight; the design gets out of its way.

A caution: the Indian mid-size sites are not a craft benchmark. 3 of 6 load slider libraries (Slick and Swiper on Technopak, Primus, Sattva), and most use free template fonts. Copying them would be the degrade the user fears. Their lesson is in the content (numbers, insight titles, plain "who we are" lines), not the look.

## 5. Two directions

### Direction A: "The partner's brief" (editorial report)

The site reads like a short, well-made research note from a firm the reader respects: the first line says what Sphyr is and for whom, sections are numbered like an executive summary, and every visual is an exhibit with a caption and a source. It keeps v2's type, warm paper and restraint, and moves the brand from the ribbon illustration to the content.

Rules:
1. Type: keep Newsreader for H1 and H2 and Manrope for everything else. Upright only in headings; italic only inside quotes.
2. Colour: warm paper ground; one deep ink (the darkest product green or a near-navy ink) for headings and rules; product green for actions only. Ribbon colours only in the logo.
3. Grid: a 12-column report grid with a narrow left margin column for section numbers in plain body-size text (not uppercase, not mono, not a kicker); body in a 7-column reading measure; exhibits run 9 or 12 columns.
4. Imagery: no illustration in the first screen. Product views become exhibits: a sentence-case caption below the frame ("Exhibit 1. A teammate's card after a project"), with the sample label as its source line. Never a label above a heading. The circle of 14 becomes a labelled data exhibit.
5. Motion: none on load. Feedback motion only (v2's 160ms rules). The core ribbon animation is removed or moved behind a "Play" control inside its exhibit.
6. Motif: the ribbon stays the logo, in the approved logo colours. At most one ribbon figure on a page, in the approved colours, started by the reader. Option for the user to approve: a tonal green version of that figure (considered and dropped on 2026-11-24).
7. Density: more structure, not more prose: short headed sections, a key-numbers row with sources, and a table for pilot terms. Still no three equal cards and the 25-word first screen target.

### Direction B: "Quiet institution" (Swiss corporate grid)

The site looks like a confident advisory or law firm site from the award galleries: white ground, a strict grid, one deep colour, a sans-led type system with one serif statement, and proof set as named lists and tables. It moves further from v2 and closer to the corporate look the professor describes, while staying at gallery level.

Rules:
1. Type: Manrope for headings and body at tighter tracking and larger sizes; Newsreader kept for one statement per page (the core line or one quote).
2. Colour: white or near-white ground (not warm paper); one deep teal or deep green as the brand colour used in large fields (a full-width band for the pilot block); no second accent.
3. Grid: visible structure: hairline rules between rows, labelled rows for "What a pilot measures", "What stays private", "What it costs", like PAX's and Hafnia's service lists.
4. Imagery: one product view in the hero, cropped tight; no ribbon art. Data shown as tables and one chart.
5. Motion: feedback only (v2's 160ms rules). No entrance animation.
6. Motif: ribbon only as logo and favicon.
7. Density: medium; proof as named lists (pilot terms, measures, privacy rules), like Barber Hauler's mandates and Setia's rankings.

References: [Setia Law](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/setia-law), [PAX Corporate Finance](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/pax-corporate-finance), [Polpharma Biologics](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/polpharma-biologics) (for the one-teal system, not its motion).

Changes from v2: palette from warm to cool white, Newsreader demoted, ribbon art removed, toggle demoted, layout from scenes to labelled rows. Stays: Manrope, product frames, sample labels, slop rules, honest copy rules.

Risk: higher degrade risk. A sans-led white corporate grid is the default of templates and AI page builders; without the warm paper and serif, the page can slide toward "Bootstrap consulting site" (Technopak, Primus). It needs very good spacing and type sizes to stay award-level, and it discards decisions from the v2 sessions.

### Lean

I lean to A. Evidence: Redseer, a homegrown Indian strategy firm in the segment's own peer set, sets display headlines in a serif over Manrope body text on warm neutrals; Bain, Oliver Wyman and Roland Berger also set headings in a serif; and Barber Hauler and Hafnia (and probably RBB) do the same on warm or quiet grounds. So v2's base is already in the consulting register; what reads as agency is the hero ribbon animation, the italic brand line, the manifesto paragraph and the agency references. A fixes those while keeping the work the user spent hours on. B is the safer answer to "look corporate" but the riskier answer to "do not degrade". This is my reading of 13 consulting, 12 vendor and 11 award sites, not a test with readers.
