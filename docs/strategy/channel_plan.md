# Channel plan: zero budget and non-invasive first

The professor's question: "If I told you that we had no money and I don't want to be a creepy, stalking, privacy-invading company, how would you change the channel list?" This plan starts from that constraint. Paid channels come last and are optional.

## Principles

1. **People choose to hear from us.** Every channel is one the reader opted into, a room they walked into, or an introduction from someone they know.
2. **No tracking of individuals.** The site and the 2-minute walkthrough use no ad pixels, no third-party trackers and no retargeting. If we count anything, it is page-level totals.
3. **Reach the champion where she already learns from peers.** Kavya trusts other heads of talent and partners more than vendors.
4. **Earn attention with something useful.** Every touch should be worth reading even if she never buys.
5. **Time it to the April round.** The window is November to January.

## Never do

- Retargeting, tracking pixels, lookalike or matched audiences.
- Scraped or bought contact lists, enrichment tools that find personal emails or phone numbers.
- Cold mass email or automated LinkedIn connection and message sequences.
- Messaging people at their firm about a named employee, or any outreach that uses personal data we were not given.
- Implying that a community, event, the professor or a university endorses Sphyr.
- Fake accounts, paid reviews, planted comments in groups.

It costs time, not money | Naming the problem, looking for answers | Kavya, Vikram | Post as someone we are not; invent first-hand stories; tag people to get reach |

| 3 | **Warm introductions** through the professor's, the team's and alumni networks: one personal note per firm, asking permission to share the walkthrough | One trusted introduction outweighs any ad. | 4 | **HR peer communities**: National HRD Network chapters (for example Delhi and NCR, about 2,300 members[^nhrdn_delhi]; Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad; plus its national conference[^nhrdn_conf]), SHRM India[^shrmi] | Where heads of talent meet peers. We attend, ask questions, and offer a talk on "calibration when people rotate" if the chapter wants one | Naming the problem, seeking validation | Kavya | Sell from the stage, collect attendee lists, sponsor without disclosure |
| 5 | **Consulting professional bodies**: the Institute of Management Consultants of India (IMCI), with chapters across India[^imci] | Where consultants and some partners meet. A talk or written piece on how firms keep track of work across engagements | Naming the problem | Vikram, partners | Imply membership or endorsement we do not have |
| 6 | **Content people choose to receive**: a monthly Sphyr note on LinkedIn or by email, sign-up only, one-click unsubscribe | Keeps the relationship useful after the first touch without chasing | All stages after first touch | Kavya | Add anyone who did not sign up; send more than monthly |
| 7 | **Our own site**: the method in public, "what Sphyr does not do", the 2-minute walkthrough, the pilot terms | The destination for every other channel. It answers the "no reviews" problem with openness | Looking for answers, seeking validation | Kavya, Vikram | Gate the walkthrough behind a form; hide the price |
| 8 | **Contributed articles** to HR and business media that accept pieces from practitioners, for example People Matters[^peoplematters] | Borrowed reach with editorial standards, aimed at Kavya | Naming the problem | Kavya | Pay for "sponsored" pieces disguised as editorial |
| 9 | **Peer references from the pilot cohort** (later) | The real replacement for reviews | Seeking validation, making the case | Kavya, Vikram | Pressure a pilot firm to be a reference; publish results without consent |

We name communities and media because they exist and fit the persona. We have no partnership with any of them. Each one is approached on its own terms, as a participant.

## Layer 2: optional small paid layer

Only if money becomes available, and only after Layer 1 has shown which message works. Spend figures are planning estimates, not quotes.

| Option | Rule | Cap (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| A fixed-fee placement in an opt-in HR newsletter, such as ETHRWorld's daily newsletter[^ethrworld] (its site lists advertising formats such as "Advertorial" and "Digital Branding & Awareness"; newsletter slots and rates not checked) | Contextual: same ad for every reader. No tracking links beyond a plain URL | One placement per month, about ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 each, to be checked |
| A small stall or session at an HR or consulting event (NHRDN chapter meet, TechHR India[^peoplematters]; SHRM India's 2026 conference ran 26 to 27 Nov 2026, before this window[^shrmi]) | Clearly marked as Sphyr. No badge scanning; people leave details only if they ask | One event per quarter, about ₹50,000 to ₹1,50,000, to be checked |
| LinkedIn sponsored post | Only job-function and industry targeting. No Insight Tag, no matched audiences, no retargeting, no lead-gen forms that prefill personal data | ₹30,000 per month for one test month, stop if no walkthroughs |

Total paid layer cap in the first quarter (estimate): about ₹1,50,000. Stop rule: if a paid option brings fewer walkthrough visits than the same month's Layer 1 channels, cut it.

## Sequence (November 2026 to March 2027)

| When | Focus | Channels |
|---|---|---|
| Late November | Name the problem before appraisal planning starts | LinkedIn posts 1 and 2, site live with the walkthrough, first warm introductions |
| December | Validation and the pilot offer | LinkedIn post 3, community sessions where invited, 20-minute calls, the ad |
| January | Close pilots that can still run before April | Calls with heads of talent and partners, the monthly note |
| February to March | Run pilots; publish the scorecard method | Monthly note, pilot check-ins |
| April to June | First calibrations; collect results with consent | References, published scorecard |

## How we know a channel works (without tracking people)

- Count walkthrough page visits per week in total, not per person.
- Ask in every 20-minute call: "Where did you hear about us?"
- Use a different short plain URL per channel if needed (for example /walkthrough on the site), not personal tracking links.
- Success for Layer 1 by the end of January (estimate to test): 3 to 5 firms in a paid pilot.
