# Positioning: product and vision

## What the feedback asked for

The submitted statement ("Sphyr helps to make feedback so frequent and effortless that, in a growing company, no appraisal depends on a single opinion or a single bad month") was "too focused on the present". A good statement is "generally a mix of both" product and vision. The professor wrote the Sphyr whitepaper, so the vision half uses his own beliefs, not a new one: career progression as "an apolitical recognition of value" rather than "a political favor"; seeing "quiet, reliable executioners"; giving employees "a voice in the assessment of peers".[^lens]

## Recommended statement

Two lines, in the shape IKEA uses (a vision, then a business idea).[^v3]

> **Vision.** Careers in consulting should be earned in front of the people who saw the work, not decided by whoever remembers it.
>
> **Product.** For homegrown consulting firms where people move from engagement to engagement, Sphyr is 360 feedback that takes two minutes every two weeks, from the people each consultant works with. Unlike a year-end review written from memory or one manager's end-of-project form, it keeps the record when the team breaks up, so partners decide on the whole year.

Vision: 21 words. Product: 57 words.

**The idea to remember** (used across every artefact, see [message guideline](message_guideline.md)):

> **The engagement ends. The evidence stays.**

### Why this one

- **It has a destination.** The vision line names a future (careers earned, quiet work seen) that people can believe in and that the professor will recognise as his.
- **The vision filters.** It rules out what Sphyr is not: not surveillance (it is about being seen by colleagues, not by a system), not a manager's tool (the people who saw the work give the view), not a bigger annual form.
- **The product line keeps the how.** Target, category, mechanism, alternative and outcome are all there, in plain words a teammate can read once.
- **It claims only what is true.** People "work with" the consultant; nobody "decides" except partners. No "anonymous", "unbiased", "everyone" or "AI".

## Runners-up

| ID | Statement | Why not first |
|---|---|---|
| B: a voice | "We believe the people you work with should shape how your work is judged, not one partner once a year. For homegrown consulting firms, Sphyr makes that practical: two minutes of feedback every two weeks, while the engagement team is still together." | Strong for consultants, weaker for the managing partner, who may hear "less say for partners" |
| C: growth | "Feedback should help consultants grow while the engagement is still running, not judge them once a year after it ends. Sphyr gives homegrown consulting firms two-minute feedback every two weeks from the people each consultant works with." | Growth is true but soft for a buyer whose pain is calibration and exits; it also leans on the trend line, which needs several cycles |

## Named alternatives and their weakness

| Alternative | What it does well | Its weakness in a consulting firm |
|---|---|---|
| The year-end review and partner calibration | Familiar; partners trust their own judgement | Written months later, from memory of the last or nearest engagement. In two large data sets, idiosyncratic rater effects explained 62% and 53% of rating variance and actual performance 21% and 25%.[^scullen] |
| One manager's end-of-project form (a shared form or spreadsheet) | Cheap, quick to start | One rater, once per engagement, often late or missing; it leaves with the engagement manager |
| The appraisal module in an Indian HRMS | Already paid for; Keka's Perform module lists 360 performance reviews[^keka] | Built around review cycles HR sets up; not designed around engagements and rotation |
| Global suites built for consulting, such as PerformYard | Real fit: "project-based review cycles", feedback "from clients, peers, and mentors", reviews aligned "to project or client engagement completion"[^performyard], and a "running record" of performance[^performyard_cf] | Review cycles are designed and scheduled by HR ("schedule review cycles on any cadence"[^performyard]), usually around project completion; there is no light default rhythm built into the method. Price is compared in the pricing work. Sphyr must not claim "project reviews", "client feedback" or "running record" as unique |

PerformYard is the closest alternative. Its consulting page answers much of the same pain. That is why Sphyr's difference has to rest on rhythm, effort, the person's own circle and fit, not on features PerformYard also lists.

## Differentiation that survives comparison

1. **A fortnightly rhythm, on by default, while the work is happening.** One organization-wide frequency; fortnightly is the default (prototype "Feedback Frequency").[^proto] The method is "about 2 minutes every two weeks" for "the people they actually worked with" (product guide p2).[^doc] Admins can pick another rhythm, as PerformYard's customers can; the difference is that a light fortnightly rhythm is the method's default, not a review cycle HR has to design per project.
2. **Two-minute cards, not forms.** How was it (was good, could improve, no interaction), a few tags, an optional note. In the 2018 paper-and-pen pilot cards took "a little over two minutes (02:09 mins)" for a sphere of 14.[^wp]
3. **Each consultant's own circle.** People keep their own sphere of peers, seniors, juniors and external people such as client counterparts, invited by email (product guide p5; prototype "My Sphyr").[^doc] The view comes from who actually worked with them.
4. **Many raters, pooled.** Ratings are normalized and pooled, and comments are anonymized to the person rated. No single voice dominates the record.
5. **Partners still decide.** Sphyr feeds calibration with the whole year; it does not rate or promote anyone.

## Value discipline

**Customer intimacy, with the segment.** Sphyr wins by fitting how homegrown consulting firms work: engagements, rotation, partner calibration, client relationships owned by partners, and the April round. Simplicity (two minutes, five clicks) serves this; it is not the discipline itself. Decisions that do not fit consulting firms of 40 to 400 are out.

## Reason to believe

- **The mechanism is visible.** The 2-minute walkthrough on our site shows the card, the sphere and the record.
- **The method was tested before the software.** A 2018 paper-and-pen pilot run by Sphyr's founder in four companies. Self-reported; not a software result.[^wp]
- **Research backs the problem.** Ratings mostly measure the rater (Scullen, Mount and Goff, 2000).[^scullen]
- **A consulting firm changed course itself.** Deloitte moved to snapshots completed "at the end of a project, phase, or at least quarterly".[^deloitte] This shows the problem is recognised in consulting. It does not mean Deloitte uses or endorses Sphyr.

## What this positioning says no to

Surveillance and productivity tracking; replacing partners, appraisals or the HRMS; claims of fairness guarantees; firms under 40 and global firms; IT consulting.
