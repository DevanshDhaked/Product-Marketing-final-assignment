# Journey map: Kavya from trigger to renewal

> Kavya, Meera, Siddharth, Vikram and all quotes are illustrative composites ([Kavya's persona](persona_head_of_talent.md)). They are not customers or testimonials.

## Where the story starts

Last June, Siddharth, a senior consultant, left for a Big 4 firm. In his exit interview he said the partner who rated him had seen three weeks of his year. This year Meera, in the Bengaluru office, is on the same path: four engagements, three engagement managers, one client CFO who said her model saved the deal, and an engagement manager who has since left. Calibration is in April. It is now late November.

Kavya is the person who has to make April different. This map follows her, not a funnel.

## How to read "Can we act on it?"

- **Yes**: Sphyr or our team can change what happens at this moment.
- **Partly**: we can help, but the outcome depends on the firm.
- **No**: a condition we cannot change. We say so, and we do not claim it. We may still meet her at the moment it becomes visible.

## Stage 1. Trigger (October to November)

It starts on a Tuesday, when a partner asks Kavya for "the file" on Meera, whom he has staffed for three weeks this year. She opens her tracker, finds two thin forms, and remembers sitting across from Siddharth in June.

*"It's happening again. I can see exactly how Meera's April will go."* She feels a mix of guilt and dread. Her stake: another April like the last one.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The firm grows to 150 people in three offices; partners can no longer see everyone's work | Notices it in small ways: partners rating people they barely know | *"Eleven partners can't know a hundred and fifty people."* | Growth itself | **No** on the growth (see below). **Yes** on the moment it shows | Content that names the moment in partner words, timed for November to January |
| A partner asks her for "the file" on a consultant he did not lead | Sends two thin project-end forms and a forwarded client email | *"This is all I have. It isn't fair to her."* | The evidence was never collected while the team was together | **Yes** | This is the exact symptom our first LinkedIn post and the site hero describe |
| She re-reads Siddharth's exit interview | Wonders who is next | *"I agreed with him. I couldn't say so."* | The exit already happened | **No** for Siddharth. **Partly** for the next one | Give her words for it: "the engagement ends, the evidence leaves with the team" |
| The bench: a consultant between engagements | Nothing to review | *"Nobody's watching her work because there isn't any."* | No work, no witnesses | **No** | Say plainly that Sphyr cannot help on the bench |

## Stage 2. Naming the problem (November)

On the train home she scrolls LinkedIn and stops at a post about consultants being rated by whoever remembers them. She reads it twice. It is the first time someone has described her April without blaming the partners.

*"It isn't that partners are unfair. It's that by April they remember the last engagement."* Relief at having words for it. Her stake: whether she can say it in partner language.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A post in her LinkedIn feed, shared by someone in her HR network | Reads it, recognises her firm, saves it | *"That's us. Someone else sees it too."* | Most content about reviews is written for 2,000-person companies | **Yes** | Founder and team posts in consulting words: engagement, calibration, partner, staffing |
| She searches "360 feedback for consulting firms" | Finds suites and HR software lists | *"These are built for companies with an HR department of fifty."* | Category language is HR jargon | **Yes** | The site speaks consulting, and says early who Sphyr is not for |

## Stage 3. Looking for answers (late November to December)

On Sunday evening she opens three tabs: her HRMS vendor's appraisal module, a global suite, and Sphyr. She gives each one ten minutes, the time she has.

*"I'll give it ten minutes. If I have to book a sales call to understand it, I'm out."* Impatient, sceptical. Her stake: her time.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Our website | Reads the hero and how it works | *"Two minutes every two weeks. Even my busiest people could do that."* | She must see it, not be told | **Yes** | The 2-minute walkthrough on our site, no form, no sign-in |

## Stage 4. Seeking validation (December)

The walkthrough made sense. Then she searches for reviews and finds nothing, asks her HR peer group and hears nothing, and feels the familiar fear of backing something no one else has tried.

*"No reviews. No customers. Am I really going to put my name on this?"* Anxious. Her stake: her credibility.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Review sites | Searches for Sphyr reviews and finds none | *"Nobody has tried this."* | A new product has no reviews | **Yes**, with substitute proof (see below) | Make the method, the evidence and the people visible |
| Her HR peer group | Asks "has anyone used Sphyr?" | *"Silence. Great."* | No peer has used it yet | **Partly** | We never plant answers. Once a pilot cohort exists, its heads of talent can choose to take calls |

She has one meeting with Vikram before the partners' offsite. She rewrites her one-page note three times, each time removing a word that sounds like HR.

*"Vikram will ask one question: will my partners hate this?"* Nervous, prepared. Her stake: her one budget ask of the year.

## Stage 6. Paid pilot (January to March)

Two practices are in. The first cycles go well. Then the strategy practice hits a client deadline week, and Kavya checks the participation number every morning before her coffee.

Setup, invitations and the first card belong to the first-use plan in `docs/packaging/`. This stage starts after the first cycle.

*"Cycle three is the real test. That's when the client deadlines hit."* Hopeful, watchful. Her stake: participation, with her name on it.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A client-heavy fortnight | Watches the participation number | *"Please don't drop below the line we agreed."* | Long client weeks | **Partly** | Cards take about two minutes; the prototype's notification copy includes "Feedback reminder: 3 reviews remaining for this fortnight cycle"[^proto]; we cannot change client workload |
| Meera opens her own insights | Kavya asks her what she thinks | *"She said it was the first time she'd seen what people thought while it still mattered."* | Consultants need a reason to keep going | **Yes** | Each person sees their own trend, strongest signals and anonymized comments |
| A consultant invites a client, with the partner's agreement | Checks the client was not bothered | *"One annoyed client and I'm done."* | Client fatigue | **Partly** | The partner agrees before the consultant sends the email invite; we advise once per engagement phase at most |

Open conflict, not resolved here: the pilot length and price come from the pricing work. A 6-week pilot is 3 fortnightly cycles, while team sources say about 6 cycles (12 weeks) are needed before the record is useful ([source conflicts, row 11](../prior_work/source_conflicts.md)).

## Stage 7. First calibration on Sphyr (April to June)

The night before calibration she opens Meera's record: four engagements, views from people on each of them, and a client counterpart the partner added in February. She does not know what the partners will decide. She knows what they will be looking at.

*"For the first time, the partner talking about Meera had seen what three other engagement managers and a client thought."* Vindicated, but careful. Her stake: whether partners see something they did not already know.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Partners open member insights before calibration | Asks each partner to open the record before the meeting | *"They actually looked."* | Partners may ignore it | **Partly** | A short guide on reading a record across cycles; the partners decide |
| A partner wants a particular outcome anyway | Watches it happen | *"A tool can't fix that."* | Favouritism | **No** | Never claim Sphyr ends favouritism |
| Meera's outcome | Waits | *"Whatever they decide, they decided on her whole year."* | We cannot promise a promotion | **No** on the outcome. **Yes** on the record | The record is there; the decision is the partners' |

## Stage 8. Renewal (after the first round)

In June she sits down with Vikram and the scorecard they agreed in January. Some numbers are good and one is not.

*"I said we'd measure it. Now I have to show him the numbers, good or bad."* Her stake: being the person who fixed April.

| Touchpoint | What she does | What she thinks and feels | Problem | Can we act on it? | What Sphyr or the team does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The pilot scorecard | Reports to Vikram | *"I want this to be honest, not a sales report."* | Proof must be the firm's own | **Yes** | The scorecard agreed before the pilot, reported as it is |

## The "outgrows what one person can see" touchpoint, answered

The professor asked of this kind of touchpoint: is it relevant, and can we do anything about it?

- **The growth itself: No.** We cannot and should not change how fast a firm grows or how many offices it opens. Calling it "relevant" hides that.
- **The moment the growth becomes visible: Yes.** Growth shows up as a partner asking for "the file", a calibration fight, an exit interview. These are moments Kavya notices and can be reached at, with content that names them in her words, from November to January.
- **The consequence: Yes, that is the product.** What growth breaks is that partners no longer see the work. Sphyr collects the view of the people who did see it every two weeks, while the team is still together.

## Social proof when there are no reviews

Kavya looks for reviews and finds none. Faking or buying them is not an option. What we can give her instead, in the order it becomes available:

1. **The method in public (day one).** A plain page on how cards, spheres and pooling work, and a list of what Sphyr does not do. A product that shows its workings asks for less trust.
2. **The 2-minute walkthrough (day one).** She can see the product herself before talking to anyone.
3. **The 2018 pilot, labelled honestly (day one).** "In a 2018 paper-and-pen pilot run by Sphyr's founder in four companies, cards took a little over two minutes (02:09) for an average sphere of 14 people."[^wp] Always say it was not the software and was self-reported.
4. **Borrowed research (day one).** Rater effects and Deloitte's per-project snapshots, cited with sources ([Vikram's persona](persona_managing_partner.md)).
5. **People she can talk to (day one).** The 20-minute call with our team.
6. **A pilot cohort with a public scorecard (first pilots).** Three to five firms. Before the pilot starts we publish what we will measure and report, for example participation by cycle 4 and the share of consultants with feedback from three or more engagements by calibration. Results are published only with each firm's consent, unnamed unless they agree.
7. **Peer references (after the cohort).** Heads of talent from the cohort who choose to take a call with someone like Kavya.

Never: invented reviews, "trusted by" logos, paid testimonials, or quotes from this file presented as real.
