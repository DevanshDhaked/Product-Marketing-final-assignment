# Persona: Vikram, managing partner

> **Read this first.** Vikram and his words are an illustrative composite. No managing partner has been interviewed. Every quote we have from the segment comes from employees, so **his language here is a hypothesis to test** in the first five conversations. Never present any of it as a testimonial.

## Who he is

Vikram is 52 and the founding and managing partner of Kavya's firm ([Kavya's persona](persona_head_of_talent.md)). He spent twelve years at a global strategy firm before starting his own. He still leads two client relationships himself. He chairs the April calibration, where eleven partners argue about forty promotions in two long days.

He built the firm on the promise of senior attention and hands-on execution. His biggest cost is people, and his biggest risk is losing the good ones to firms that pay more.

## What keeps him up

- **Good juniors leaving.** Each senior consultant who leaves takes client knowledge and a year of training with them, and the replacement costs months.
- **Calibration fights.** Two partners describe the same person in opposite terms because each saw a different engagement. He settles it with gut feel and knows it.
- **"Partiality" in exit interviews.** It hurts because it is about his firm's fairness, which he sells to clients and recruits on.
- **Partner time.** Anything that adds admin for partners will not survive.

## What he needs to hear to sign

In this order:

1. **"Your partners still decide. They just see the whole year."** Sphyr feeds calibration; it does not rate anyone for him.
2. **"The record is built while the engagement team is still together."** Two minutes, every two weeks, from the people who worked with the consultant, including the client when the partner chooses.
3. **"It costs partners almost nothing."** Partners fill the same two-minute cards as everyone else. In Sphyr a manager is treated as another member of the sphere (whitepaper p5).
4. **"You can test it before April with a small paid pilot."** One or two practices, with a success test agreed in advance.
5. **"Here is why it should work."** The method in one page, a reason from research, and the honest 2018 pilot.

## Proof he accepts, and proof he does not

| Accepts | Does not accept |
|---|---|
| A peer, another managing partner, on a phone call | Logos, star ratings, "trusted by" claims |
| Clicking through the 2-minute walkthrough himself | A sales deck |
| Research he would cite to a client: idiosyncratic rater effects explained 62% and 53% of rating variance in two large data sets, actual performance only 21% and 25%[^scullen] | Vague "studies show" lines |
| A consulting firm that changed its own system: Deloitte moved to "snapshots" completed "at the end of a project, phase, or at least quarterly"[^deloitte] | Claims that Deloitte uses or endorses Sphyr (it does not) |
| The 2018 pilot, stated exactly: a paper-and-pen version of the method run by Sphyr's founder in four companies; cards took a little over two minutes (02:09) for an average sphere of 14[^wp] | Pilot percentages presented as software results |
| A scorecard from his own pilot | Promises of "fairer" or "unbiased" promotions |

## Questions he will ask (and the honest answer)

- **"Will my partners have to change how they calibrate?"** No. They get a fuller record of each person to bring to the same discussion.
- **"Will my clients be asked to rate my people?"** Only if the partner adds them. External people are invited by email into a consultant's sphere; the partner decides.
- **"Is it anonymous?"** Ratings are pooled and comments are anonymized to the person rated. Managers approve requests to edit submitted feedback and can see who asked. Engagement teams are small, so be careful with the word.
- **"Does it work with our HR system?"** Members are imported from a file. There are no integrations. It runs alongside whatever the firm uses today.
- **"Who else uses it?"** Nobody yet. Say so, then offer the pilot and the scorecard.
- **"What does it cost?"** From the pricing work. Do not quote a number before it is approved.

## What makes him say no

- A partner says, "So now HR rates my team?"
- Participation drops below the agreed threshold in a client-heavy month.
- A client complains about being asked.
- The pilot shows partners nothing they did not already know.
- Anything that sounds like surveillance or an HR suite.

## What he must never hear from us

"Replace your appraisal", "AI decides promotions", "objective", "ends favouritism", "track productivity", any invented customer or partner firm, and any line that implies the professor, a university or an industry body endorses Sphyr.
