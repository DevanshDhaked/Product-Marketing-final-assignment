# Segment: homegrown Indian management consulting firms

Course-folder paths such as `tasks/saas/...` are relative to `sem_3/pm/`, outside this repo.

## Definition (confirmed by the team on 2026-11-24)

**Homegrown, partner-led Indian management and strategy consulting firms with about 40 to 400 professionals.** Their consultants rotate across several engagements a year, and partners decide promotions once a year.

Strong signals, not filters:
- Several practices (for example strategy, operations, public sector).
- Offices in two or more of Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad, or consultants staffed far from the partner who rates them.
- A head of talent or HR lead exists, but there is no large HR function and no global performance system.

Excluded: IT services and tech consulting (another team's domain).

## Why the feedback problem is sharpest here

In a product company a person mostly works for one manager, who sees the work. In a consulting firm the work is split into engagements and the person who rates you often did not see most of it.

1. **Work comes in short engagements.** "A typical engagement runs six to twelve weeks, answers one defined question, and ends in a recommendation to senior management."[^strategycase]
2. **People rotate.** One consultant told The Finance Story that at EY India they were "actively deployed on 4 to 5 projects" in a year.[^financestory] Each engagement can have a different engagement manager and partner.
3. **The work happens at the client.** The client counterpart often sees more of the consultant's daily work than the partner who will rate them (team research).[^pain]
4. **One decision a year, made in calibration.** Indian firms follow the April to March financial year; Deloitte India "normally declare[s] in May".[^financestory] Consulting reviews "usually combine project feedback, self-assessment, counselor or development-manager input, calibration discussions, and formal ratings", and "many firms compare employees at similar levels during calibration sessions".[^umbrex]
5. **Partner memory decides.** By calibration, the people who saw a consultant's best work (an earlier engagement manager, a client, a peer) are on other engagements or have left. The partner rates the consultant they remember, usually from the last engagement or from proximity.
6. **The bench.** Between engagements nobody sees the work. The Big 4 in India left "more than 1000 mid-level professionals" on the bench in 2023.[^financestory] Sphyr cannot help here (see [journey map](journey_map.md)).

What employees of homegrown firms write (verbatim, unattributed in public copy):
- "Partiality is rampant. If you become a yesman to the leadership they might still think of giving you a promotion" (one review among 197 for one homegrown firm).[^ambition_primus]
- "Firm gives good visibility and learning opportunities since partners are closely involved in the studies" (one review among 264 for another).[^ambition_avalon] This is the positive case: when the partner is close to the work, the problem disappears. It is also why the lower size edge sits near 40.

Two reviews out of 461 are illustrations, not a measure of how common the problem is.

## Why homegrown firms, and why now

- **They are growing.** The India management consulting services market is expected to grow from USD 9.36 billion in 2026 to USD 18.93 billion by 2032, and small and medium-sized enterprises are "the fastest-growing segment" (13.05% forecast CAGR).[^mordor] The Hindu (2025) describes a "new generation of homegrown firms" winning work by offering "hands-on execution and shared accountability for results".[^hindu]
- **They compete for the same talent as MBB and the Big 4, usually at lower pay.** "Pay isn't as high" appears in the same Avalon review quoted above.[^ambition_avalon] They cannot win people on pay or brand. A promotion system people trust is one of the few levers they control.
- **Growth breaks partner memory.** A firm of 40 can rely on partners knowing everyone. A firm of 150 across three cities cannot. Primus Partners went from founding in 2019 to "400+ professionals, 240+ ongoing projects, and a presence across 18 Indian states and 14+ countries" in seven years.[^primus]
- **Timing.** Promotions land April to June. Sphyr needs several fortnightly cycles before the record is useful, so the selling window is November to January. On 24 November 2026 we are at the start of that window.

## Size edges and confidence

| Edge | Reasoning | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Lower, about 40 | Below this, partners work on most studies themselves (see the Avalon review above). SPI's 2025 benchmark showed voluntary attrition stepping up from 6.1% (10 to 30 staff) to 7.7% (31 to 100).[^spi] | Low to medium. SPI is global and US-heavy, and was read in October, not re-read. |
| Upper, about 400 | Primus Partners at "400+ professionals" marks the top of the homegrown band.[^primus] Above this, firms build HR functions and buy suites; the Big 4 and MBB run global systems and procurement. | Medium. |

What would move the band: interviews showing 20-person firms already rotate staff across many partners (lower the floor), or 300-person firms already running per-engagement reviews well (lower the ceiling).

## How many firms (estimate)

No source gives a count of Indian management consulting firms with 40 to 400 professionals. This is our estimate, marked as a guess.

- **Method.** Count firms, not market value. Combine (a) firms named as homegrown in press and career guides (for example Redseer, Praxis Global Alliance, Avalon Consulting and Vector Consulting Group[^roadtooffer], Primus Partners[^primus]); (b) consulting recruiters listed in public campus placement reports of the top business schools; (c) a manual LinkedIn company search (industry "Management Consulting", India, 51 to 200 and 201 to 500 employees), read by a person, not scraped; then remove IT consulting, staffing firms and Indian offices of global firms.
- **Guess until the count is done:** about 150 to 400 firms in the band. At an average of about 120 professionals, that is roughly 18,000 to 48,000 seats. These numbers are guesses for planning and must not appear in public copy.
- **What matters more than the total:** the first 3 to 5 firms. The plan in [acquisition and engagement](acquisition_engagement.md) needs a small cohort, not the whole market.

## Anti-segment (do not pursue)

- Firms under about 40 people, where partners see everyone.
- MBB, the Big 4 and other global firms, with global systems and procurement.
- IT services and tech consulting.
- Staffing or body-shop "consulting", where people sit at one client for years and the client's own manager reviews them.
- Solo advisors and expert networks.

## How to find them without scraped lists

The [channel plan](channel_plan.md) bans scraped lists, bought data and cold mass email. Finding firms follows the same rule.

- Read what firms publish about themselves: websites, press releases, foundation-day news, partner bylines in business media.
- Public campus placement reports, which name consulting recruiters.
- Industry bodies whose events and member pages are public, such as the Institute of Management Consultants of India (see [channel plan](channel_plan.md)).
- Warm introductions: alumni networks, the professor's network, and partners who already know each other.
- People who come to us: readers of our posts, people who take the 2-minute walkthrough, attendees who ask to stay in touch.

A person reads and chooses each firm. Nobody is contacted who has not either asked to hear from us or been introduced.
