# Message guideline for copywriters and designers

Use this file to write or design anything for Sphyr. If a line you want to write is not covered here, check the [do-not-claim list](../product/do_not_claim.md) and ask before publishing.

## 1. The core idea

> **The engagement ends. The evidence stays.**

What it means, in one sentence: Sphyr collects the view of the people who saw a consultant's work, every two weeks, while the engagement team is still together, so partners decide promotions on the whole year and not the last engagement.

The vision behind it ([positioning](positioning.md)): careers earned in front of the people who saw the work.

## 2. What to say, always in this order

| Step | Say | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The moment | The engagement ended; the people who saw the work moved on | "By April, the people who saw Meera's best work are on three other engagements." |
| 2. The mechanism | Two minutes, every two weeks, from the people they work with | "Every two weeks, each person spends about two minutes on short cards for the people they worked with." |
| 3. The result | At calibration partners see the whole year, and still decide | "In April, partners calibrate on the whole year, not the last engagement." |
| 4. The vision | Careers earned in front of the people who saw the work | "Because careers should be earned in front of the people who saw the work." |
| 5. The next step | The 2-minute walkthrough | "See it in the 2-minute walkthrough." |

Short formats: never drop steps 1 and 5. An ad may use 1, 3 and 5 only. A LinkedIn post may stop at 1 and 2 and ask a question. The website uses all five.

**Calls to action.** Primary: **"the 2-minute walkthrough"** (demo.html on our site). Never call it "live demo" or "free trial". Second step: **a 20-minute call** between our team and the firm's head of talent. To the head of talent: "Book a 20-minute call". To a partner: "Ask your head of talent to book a 20-minute call". One call to action per piece.

## 3. Claims and the proof allowed with each

| Claim | Proof you may pair with it | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| The evidence leaves with the team | The Meera story, labelled as an example; employee review quotes used unattributed | "Here is how it usually goes." Never "a client told us". |
| Ratings measure the rater | Idiosyncratic rater effects explained 62% and 53% of rating variance in two large data sets; actual performance 21% and 25%[^scullen] | Cite "Scullen, Mount and Goff, 2000". Never "studies show". |
| People doubt their reviews | 29% of employees strongly agree their reviews are fair, 26% that they are accurate (Gallup)[^gallup] | Cite Gallup. It is a global figure, not consulting or India. |
| It takes about two minutes | 2018 pilot: "a little over two minutes (02:09 mins)" for an average sphere of 14[^wp] | Always: "in a 2018 paper-and-pen pilot run by Sphyr's founder". |
| Consulting already knows this | Deloitte's snapshots "at the end of a project, phase, or at least quarterly"[^deloitte] | Never imply Deloitte uses or endorses Sphyr. |
| Clients can give a view | External people are invited by email into a consultant's sphere (product guide p5; prototype "My Sphyr")[^proto] | Always "when the partner adds them". Never a headline. |
| Each person sees their own record | Prototype member "Insights": trend over cycles, strongest signal, areas to improve, anonymized comments[^proto] | Show the real screen. |
| It works for firms like yours | **No proof yet.** | Do not claim it. Offer the pilot and its public scorecard instead. |

## 4. Never say

| Never | Say instead |
|---|---|
| Anonymous, fully anonymous, untraceable | "Ratings are pooled and comments are anonymized to the person rated." |
| Unbiased, objective, fair promotions, ends favouritism, stops politics | "Partners see the whole year, not one engagement." |
| Replaces appraisals, partners, HR or your HRMS | "Feeds your existing calibration." |
| AI, smart, intelligent insights | Describe what it does: cards, pooling, trends. |
| Real-time | "Every two weeks." |
| Tracks, monitors, measures productivity | "Collects the view of the people they worked with." |
| Integrates with Keka, Darwinbox, Slack, Teams, SSO | "Import your people from a file." |
| Trusted by, our customers, loved by, reviews, ratings, logos | Nothing. We have no customers yet. Offer the pilot. |
| Sphyr customers see 94% fairness / 88% less resistance | Pilot percentages are not software results. Use only 02:09, attributed. |
| Who Should We Promote? | It is described in the product guide, not built in the prototype. Leave it out. |
| Any endorsement by the professor, IIIT Hyderabad, IMCI, NHRDN or any firm | Nothing. Name communities only as places we take part in. |
| Mobile app, Slack bot, journal, coaching, pulse surveys | Roadmap or not available. Leave out. |

## 5. Tone

- **A peer partner talking**, not HR software selling. Calm, specific, a little dry.
- **Concrete over clever.** Name the engagement, the April round, the client CFO. No abstract nouns like "talent excellence".
- **Honest about limits.** Saying what Sphyr does not do builds trust with this audience.
- **No urgency tricks.** No countdowns, "limited spots", or fear about losing people.
- Simple English. No em dashes, no double dashes, no emojis, no hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, unlock, supercharge).

## 6. Vocabulary

| Use (consulting words) | Avoid (HR jargon) |
|---|---|
| engagement, engagement team, engagement manager | project resource, team member allocation |
| partner, managing partner, practice | stakeholder, leadership team |
| calibration, the April round, promotion round | performance cycle management |
| staffing, rotation, the bench | workforce deployment |
| the people who saw the work | raters, reviewers, 360 respondents |
| record, the whole year | data, analytics, insights platform |
| two minutes every two weeks | continuous performance management |
| their circle (the product calls it "My Sphyr") | sphere of influence, ONA, VBA, sphere score |

Product terms you may use with the head of talent only, once explained: "sphere", "cycle", "the seven parameters".

## 7. One message, three listeners

| Listener | Lead with | Do not lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Kavya, head of talent | Less chasing, one record per consultant at calibration, fewer "nobody saw my work" exits | Features list, admin settings |
| Vikram, managing partner | Partners still decide, on the whole year; a small paid pilot before April | HR process, dashboards |
| Meera, consultant | The people who saw your work get a say; two minutes; you see your own record | Anything about ratings of you or monitoring |

## 8. Headline formulas, with three worked examples each

**Web hero.** Formula: *[the moment] + [what stays]*, or *[the April fact] + [the turn]*.
1. "The engagement ends. The evidence stays."
2. "In April your partners will calibrate 140 people. Each partner saw only part of their year."
3. "Promotions decided on the whole year, not the last engagement."

**LinkedIn post opener** (first two lines, before "see more"). Formula: *a scene the reader has lived*, *a sourced number*, or *a question in partner words*. Never a made-up first-hand story.
1. "Your partner asks for 'the file' on a consultant he never staffed. What do you send him?"
2. "In two large data sets, more than half of the variation in performance ratings came from who did the rating. In consulting, that person may have seen one engagement out of four."
3. "Every April, someone in your firm is rated by the partner who remembers them, not the one who saw their best work."

**Ad.** Formula: *[benefit in partner words] + [mechanism in 8 words or fewer] + [call to action]*.
1. "Calibrate on the whole year. Two minutes, every two weeks. See the 2-minute walkthrough."
2. "The engagement ends. The evidence stays. Two-minute feedback from the people who saw the work. Take the 2-minute walkthrough."
3. "Before April: who saw Meera's best work? Sphyr keeps their view. The 2-minute walkthrough at [our site]."

## 9. Visual rules

**Show:**
- **The core visual:** a timeline of four or five engagements that fade out one by one, with one continuous line (the consultant's record) running through all of them and still clear at the April calibration.
- Real prototype screens or faithful faux UI of them: the card with "Was good / Could improve / No interaction", "My Sphyr" circles, the member trend line. Use the prototype's real labels. Replace demo names (John Smith, Maya Chen, Northstar) with neutral placeholders; they are not real people or clients.
- Consulting settings: a client workroom, a whiteboard after a workshop, a calibration table seen from the side, people working, not being judged.
- The number "2 minutes" and the word "fortnightly" as visual anchors.

**Never show:**
- Faces with scores, rankings or traffic-light labels on people.
- Eyes, magnifying glasses, cameras, radar sweeps or anything that reads as surveillance.
- Star ratings, review badges, "trusted by" logo strips, invented testimonial cards.
- The prototype's "Members Needing Support" list or any screen that names a low performer.
- AI sparkles, robots, glowing brains.
- Stock handshakes and generic office smiles.

Colours, type and components come from the design work in `docs/design/` and must support both light and dark themes.

## 10. Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Ratings are pooled and comments are anonymized to the person rated." | "100% anonymous." |
| "Partners still decide. They see the whole year." | "Let the data decide promotions." |
| "In a 2018 paper-and-pen pilot run by Sphyr's founder, cards took about two minutes." | "Sphyr users finish in two minutes." |
| "Clients can add their view when the partner invites them." | "Get your clients to rate your consultants." |
| "We have no customers yet. Here is our pilot and what we will publish." | "Join leading consulting firms." |
| "Two minutes, every two weeks." | "Real-time continuous performance intelligence." |
| Label every persona and story as an example. | Present Kavya, Meera or Vikram as real. |

## 11. Before you publish, check

1. The core idea or step 1 is in the first line.
2. One call to action, worded as in section 2.
3. Every number has its source and the exact qualifier from section 3.
4. Nothing from section 4 appears, including in image text.
5. No dashes used as punctuation, no emojis.
