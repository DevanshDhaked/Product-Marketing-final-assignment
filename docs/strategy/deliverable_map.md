# Deliverable map: one message, six artefacts

The brief asks that "all the deliverables must communicate the same underlying marketing idea" in ways that suit each format, and that the artefacts help the customer "move from awareness to interest, consideration, and action".[^brief] This file assigns each artefact one job in [Kavya's journey](journey_map.md). Copy rules are in the [message guideline](message_guideline.md).

## Central message

> **The engagement ends. The evidence stays.**

Sphyr collects the view of the people who saw a consultant's work, two minutes every two weeks, while the engagement team is still together, so partners decide on the whole year. Vision: careers earned in front of the people who saw the work ([positioning](positioning.md)).

Primary call to action everywhere: **the 2-minute walkthrough** (demo.html on our site). Second step: a 20-minute call with the firm's head of talent.

## How the six work together

1. LinkedIn post 1 and the ad make Kavya recognise the problem.
2. LinkedIn post 2 shows the mechanism and sends her to the walkthrough.
3. The website lets her see it, check it and share it with the partners.
4. LinkedIn post 3 answers "nobody has used this" with the public pilot scorecard.
5. The propaganda strategy is the reasoning behind all of the above.

## The propaganda strategy, in outline

The deliverable must state its audience, the belief it wants, the message and reasoning, the tactics and channels, why they fit, how it connects to positioning, and the expected response.[^brief] Inputs for whoever writes it:

- **Audience:** Kavya (primary), Vikram (secondary), consultants as the people whose trust decides whether it works.
- **Desired belief:** "Losing the record when teams disband is a fixable problem, and fixing it gives partners more to judge with, not less."
- **Tactics:** naming the problem with one memorable phrase; choosing the enemy carefully (rotation and memory, never partners); repetition of the core idea across every channel; transparency as a persuasion tactic (method, limits, what we do not do); proof that is built in public (the scorecard); peer voice over vendor voice.
- **Ethical line:** no fear about losing people, no false scarcity, no fake social proof, no endorsement implied.
- **Expected response:** walkthrough visits, 20-minute calls, 3 to 5 paid pilots before April (estimate to test).

## Ad medium: proposal for gate 1a

The brief needs "one advertisement designed for an appropriate medium and audience"; it does not have to be on LinkedIn. Under the zero-budget, non-invasive constraint:

| Candidate | Cost | Non-invasive | Reaches Kavya or Vikram in the right mindset | Fits November to January | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Printed poster (A2) with a matching A6 handout card** at HR and consulting community events, such as NHRDN chapter meets[^nhrdn_delhi] and IMCI events[^imci] | Printing only, under ₹5,000 (estimate) | Yes: nobody is tracked; the QR code opens a plain URL | Yes: she is there to learn from peers about people problems | Yes: chapter meets run through the season | Needs each organiser's permission; small reach |
| B. Fixed-fee display slot in an opt-in HR newsletter, such as ETHRWorld's daily newsletter[^ethrworld] | Paid (Layer 2); fee not checked | Yes if contextual and without tracking links | Yes, for heads of talent | Yes | Costs money; slot availability and rules not verified |
| C. LinkedIn single-image sponsored post, job-function targeting only | Paid | Partly: the platform tracks users even if we do not | Yes | Yes | Weakest fit with the no-tracking stance |

**Recommendation: A, the event poster with a matching handout card.** It costs almost nothing, tracks nobody, and meets Kavya in a room she chose to be in, among peers, which is where she trusts what she hears. The same design can run as option B later if a paid layer opens, so no work is lost. The team decides at gate 1a.

Ad content direction (for the ad writer and designer):
- Headline: "The engagement ends. The evidence stays."
- One line: "Two minutes every two weeks, from the people who saw the work. Partners calibrate on the whole year."
- Call to action: "Take the 2-minute walkthrough" with the short URL and a QR code.
- Visual: the core visual from the message guideline. No faces, no scores, no logos of others.
- Price: mention only if the pricing work is approved, and then exactly as approved.

## Dependencies

- **Pricing work** (`docs/pricing/`): price, pilot amount and length, what happens without conversion. The website, post 3 and any ad mention of the pilot wait for it.
- **First-use work** (`docs/packaging/`): what happens from purchase to the first card. The website's "what happens after you sign" section uses it.
- **Design work** (`docs/design/`): the core visual, faux UI of real prototype screens, light and dark themes.
