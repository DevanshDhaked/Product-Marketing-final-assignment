# Anticipation plan: what each person looks forward to before the first card

> Kavya, Vikram and Meera are illustrative composites. Draft copy uses [brackets] for anything the firm fills in. Every moment is labelled **Product** or **Team service**.

## Why reassurance was not enough

The graded packaging told people Sphyr was safe and easy. The professor's point: a person can feel reassured about receiving something without looking forward to it. This plan gives each audience a specific thing to look forward to, a date for it, and something to do while they wait.

Out of scope here: price, the order, refunds, renewal and any persuasion to buy.

## What the research suggests (analogy, not proof)

- People get real enjoyment from waiting for an experience, and waiting for an experience is more pleasant and exciting than waiting for an object. Four studies, including a large experience-sampling study; sample sizes were not retrieved.[^kumar]
- Savoring an upcoming experience raises enjoyment both while it happens and when it is remembered. Five studies; sample sizes were not retrieved.[^chun]

Both are about consumer experiences, not workplace software. We use them only as design rules: give people a concrete picture of what is coming and a date, and let them do something before it starts. We do not claim they predict anything about Sphyr.

## Three rules for every moment

1. **A date, not "soon".** The admin's schedule preview gives the real cycle dates.[^proto]
2. **A picture of what arrives.** Name what the person will see, in their words. Never promise in week two what needs several cycles: the trend line comes later, the voice comes first.[^msg]
3. **Something to do while waiting.** People who choose their circle before cycle 1 have already started.

## The five moments

| # | Moment | Who | When | What they are told they get back, and when | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Kickoff and the "your April starts" card | Kavya | Cycle 0 | Her firm's words on the card today; participation by team at the end of cycle 1; each consultant's trend after a few cycles; a whole-year record in April | Team service, using the product's schedule preview and Organization VBAs |
| B | Partner note and the April preview | Partners, engagement managers | End of cycle 0 | A view of their current engagement team from the end of cycle 1; each person's record across all engagements in April | Team service, using the Member Insights screen on labelled sample data |
| C | The consultant invite | Consultants | End of cycle 0 | Notes written to them by people they worked with, and what those people noticed most, starting after the first cycle; a trend after a few cycles; partners reading their whole year in April | Team service (we draft, the managing partner sends) |
| D | Choosing the circle | Consultants | Before cycle 1 opens | They decide who gets a say in their year | Product: My Sphyr[^proto] |
| E | The first deck opens | Everyone | Cycle 1, day 1 | Two minutes; the people they worked with hear how it went; their circle is filling cards for them too | Product ("FEEDBACK THIS FORTNIGHT"), plus a short team-drafted note |

Timing caveat for A, B, C and E: no source says when a person's Insights page or a team's Team Insights first shows results, or how many raters they need. "After the first cycle" is our assumption. Confirm it against the product before any copy goes out.

## A. Kickoff and the "your April starts" card (Kavya)

In the kickoff, Kavya types her firm's values into the product, presses "Save & cascade" and sees them as tags on a sample card.[^proto] She agrees the go-live date with our team and reads the cycle dates from the schedule preview. The prototype shows the frequency choice and the resulting dates, not a start-date control. She leaves with one card from our team:

> **Your April starts on [date].**
>
> That's the [day] [number] people open their first card, written in your firm's own words.
>
> Two weeks later you'll see how many filled it in, team by team, without chasing anyone.
>
> A few cycles in, each consultant has a line showing how their work is landing.
>
> In April the partners open a record of the whole year, built two minutes at a time by the people who were there.

## B. Partner note and the April preview (partners, engagement managers)

Sent by the managing partner, drafted by us:

> **Subject: What you'll have open in April**
>
> [Name],
>
> Every April we end up rating people we saw for a few weeks of their year. This year we'll have more to go on.
>
> From [date], everyone in [practice] spends about two minutes every two weeks on short cards for the people they work with. You fill them too, for your team.
>
> From the end of that first fortnight, you'll see how your current engagement team is landing, while the engagement is still running. And at calibration, you'll open each person's record and see what the people who worked with them said at the time, on every engagement, not only yours.
>
> The decisions stay with you. You'll just be making them on the whole year.
>
> Kavya will show what the April view looks like at [day]'s partners' meeting. Ten minutes.
>
> [Managing partner]

**The April preview** (ten minutes at the partners' meeting, team service). Screen one, marked "Sample data": one consultant's Member Insights, built from people on all four of their engagements. The screen pools results; it does not split them by engagement. Screen two: the same view for their own practice, empty. Caption for screen two:

> This is [practice] today. It starts filling on [date].

Partners see the record they will have in April, and that it starts filling in a few days.

## C. The consultant invite (consultants)

Sent by the managing partner, drafted by us. It leads with what the consultant gets, not with how ratings work.

> **Subject: Who saw your best work this year?**
>
> Think about the work you're proudest of this year. Who was in the room? Probably your engagement manager, and maybe the senior who went through your model with you at midnight.
>
> From [date], those people get a say in your record.
>
> Before then, open Sphyr and add the people you work with to your circle. Every two weeks you'll get a short deck of cards, one per person: how did working with them go, what stood out, and a note if you want to leave one. About two minutes. While you fill in theirs, they're filling in yours.
>
> What comes back to you: notes from the people you worked with, and the things they noticed most. The person you write about won't see your name, and you won't see theirs. Ratings are pooled before anyone sees them. After a few cycles you'll have a line that shows how your work is landing, early enough to do something about it.
>
> And in April, the partners will have what your circle saw all year in front of them.
>
> [Managing partner]

Why this order: the professor's own words for what employees gain are "a voice in the assessment of peers" and "insights into their blind spots".[^wp] The invite opens with the voice, then the exchange, then what comes back.

## D. Choosing the circle (consultants)

The product step is "My Sphyr": "Keep this circle focused on people you work with regularly."[^proto] A short follow-up from Kavya two days before cycle 1 (team service):

> Your circle is the people who get a say in your year. Add everyone you've worked with since [month] who's still here: engagement managers, peers from each team, the juniors you've been bringing up.
>
> If a client lead saw a lot of your work, talk to your partner. From the second cycle, with the partner's OK, you can invite them by email.

## E. The first deck opens (everyone)

The product shows "FEEDBACK THIS FORTNIGHT" with one card per person in the circle and a counter such as "4 of 17".[^proto] A short note from Kavya on the morning cycle 1 opens (team service):

> **Subject: Your first deck is open**
>
> It's [date]. Your first cards are waiting in Sphyr, one for each person in your circle. Two minutes, and the people you worked with this fortnight hear how it went.
>
> They're getting a card for you too.

## Never in this copy

"Anonymous" without qualification; "the system decides"; "track"; "real-time"; any score or trend about an individual promised for the first fortnight; clients rating in cycle 1. Full list: [do-not-claim](../product/do_not_claim.md).
