Split hero

Copy left, a real product view right. Headline at most two lines, subtext at most 20 words, one primary and one secondary button. No eyebrow, no glow, no tagline under the buttons.

Buttons and links

Controls use the 8px radius. One primary button per view. Labels are one to three words and never wrap on desktop. Press shrinks the button slightly; reduced motion removes it.

Inline links look like this: how ratings are pooled. They keep an underline so they do not rely on colour alone.

Document column

For reasoning the reader must follow, such as the pricing logic or the propaganda strategy. One column at about 68 characters per line, plain headings, real lists.

Why the price is per employee

Specimen paragraph. The document column carries an argument from start to end. Each paragraph makes one point, and numbers carry their source in the same sentence or in a table below.

What the column holds

  • Headings that say the point, not a category.
  • Short paragraphs, one claim each.
  • A table or a stat when a number needs a source.

Labelled rows

For a sequence or a set of answers. The label is the content itself, never "Step 1". Stacks to one column on phones.

Pick the people
Each member keeps a small circle of peers, seniors, juniors and external contacts they work with.
Rate in a few clicks
Every fortnight, one tap per person: was good, could improve, or no interaction. Tags and a comment are optional.
Read the pooled view
Members see trends and anonymized comments. Managers see team patterns and approve edit requests.

Cards

Hairline, 12px radius, no shadow. Use an unequal pair, never three equal cards. Tint a card only when the colour carries meaning: green for a strength, amber for a caution.

Plain card

For a grouped answer that needs its own boundary. Most content does not need a card; spacing and a rule are enough.

Strength

Uses the product's positive colours.

Caution

Uses the product's could-improve colours, for limits and honest caveats.

Table

Sentence-case headers, tabular numbers aligned right, one rule per row, source in the caption. Scrolls sideways inside its frame on phones.

Quote and stat

The quote borrows the product's own anonymized feedback item. The stat shows one number with its label and source; a number without a source is not shown.

Chips, labels and notes

Questions

Native details and summary. The question is the heading; the answer is short and plain.

Can the person rated see who wrote a comment?
No. Comments are anonymized to the person rated. Their manager sees who gave feedback only when approving an edit request.
How often does feedback run?
Every fortnight by default. An admin can change the frequency.
