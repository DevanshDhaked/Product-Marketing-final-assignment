# Final Marketing Assignment

**Weightage:** 70% of the course assessment\
**Submission deadline:** 23:59, 27 November\
**Submission format:** One website link per team

## 1. Assignment brief

Your team must create a set of connected marketing artefacts for the
product or brand you have been working on.

The submission must contain:

1.  **A website** that presents the product or brand and acts as the
    central point from which the other deliverables can be accessed.
2.  **One propaganda strategy** that explains how you will shape
    audience perception and persuade the intended segment.
3.  **Three LinkedIn posts** designed for the intended audience and
    purpose.
4.  **One advertisement** designed for an appropriate medium and
    audience.

The website is the entry point to your submission. Include clear links
to the other deliverables on the website. You may place the links
directly on the main page or make them available through a menu or other
clear navigation. The evaluator should be able to find and open every
deliverable without having to search for it.

## 1.1 The product: Spyhr

The product for this assignment is **Spyhr**, a SaaS application for performance appraisal. Your team should develop the marketing work around Spyhr, using the product information and prototype link shared with you in class.

You are expected to understand the product before deciding how to market it. Use the supplied product details and prototype to identify its capabilities, value, intended users, and the problems it is designed to address. Do not invent features, benefits, integrations, or capabilities that are not supported by the information provided.

Your team must make its own reasoned marketing choices: which customer segment to prioritise, what problem or need to focus on, how to position Spyhr, and how to communicate its value. Different teams may choose different segments or angles, provided their choices are supported by the product and are explained clearly.

## 3. What the assignment is intended to assess

This assignment carries 70% weightage. It is intended to test how
effectively you can bring together the concepts and methods covered in
the course and apply them to a coherent marketing effort.

Your work should demonstrate that you can:

### Understand and address a customer segment

-   Identify the intended customer segment and make deliberate choices
    about whom you are trying to reach.
-   Use the customer persona to guide the message, language, content,
    channel, and call to action.
-   Show that you understand the customer's needs, motivations,
    concerns, and likely decision-making process.

### Use the customer journey to improve the experience

-   Consider what the customer needs at different stages of the journey.
-   Make it clear how the artefacts help the customer move from
    awareness to interest, consideration, and action.
-   Avoid treating each deliverable as an isolated piece of
    communication.

### Establish clear positioning

-   Communicate what the product or brand stands for.
-   Make the intended value and differentiation understandable.
-   Ensure that the positioning is relevant to the chosen customer
    segment.

### Connect every artefact to one central marketing message

All the deliverables must communicate the same underlying marketing
idea. They should do so in ways that suit their respective formats and
purposes.

For example, a website may explain the value in greater detail, a
LinkedIn post may introduce a problem or provoke discussion, and an
advertisement may communicate a focused benefit. The wording and
creative treatment can differ, but the underlying promise should remain
consistent.

### Apply the pricing and packaging decisions

-   Ensure that the way the product is presented is consistent with the
    pricing and packaging strategy developed during the course.
-   Make the value offered understandable.
-   Avoid making claims or promises that conflict with the product,
    price, or package.

### Plan customer acquisition and engagement

-   Show how the marketing effort is expected to attract the intended
    customers.
-   Give customers a meaningful next step.
-   Consider how the brand will maintain the relationship after the
    first interaction. Customer engagement is not limited to getting a
    click, enquiry, or purchase; it includes keeping the relationship
    useful and active over time.

## 4. Guidance for each deliverable

### A. Website

The website should communicate the product or brand clearly to the
intended customer.

It should:

-   Make the intended audience and the product's value clear.
-   Communicate the positioning and central marketing message.
-   Present relevant information in an order that makes sense to the
    customer.
-   Reflect the customer persona and the customer's likely journey.
-   Include appropriate calls to action.
-   Provide clear, working links to the propaganda strategy, all three
    LinkedIn posts, and the advertisement.

The website is also the submission hub. Do not submit a website that
looks complete but leaves the other deliverables inaccessible.

### B. Propaganda strategy

Develop one coherent strategy for influencing how the intended audience
thinks about, understands, or responds to the product or brand.

Explain:

-   The audience you intend to influence.
-   The perception, belief, attitude, or response you want to encourage.
-   The central message and the reasoning behind it.
-   The tactics and channels you will use.
-   Why those tactics are appropriate for the audience and the
    objective.
-   How the strategy connects to the positioning and the overall
    marketing message.
-   What action or response you expect from the audience.

The strategy must explain the thinking behind the approach. A list of
slogans, posts, or promotional activities without a clear objective and
rationale is not sufficient.

### C. Three LinkedIn posts

Create three distinct LinkedIn posts for the intended audience.

The posts should:

-   Have a clear purpose rather than repeat the same message three
    times.
-   Use language and content suited to LinkedIn and the intended
    audience.
-   Connect to the central marketing message while approaching it from
    different angles where appropriate.
-   Reflect the positioning and customer persona.
-   Have a clear and suitable call to action, where relevant.

The three posts should work together as part of the larger marketing
effort.

### D. One advertisement

Create one advertisement for a suitable medium and audience.

The advertisement should:

-   Communicate a clear message quickly.
-   Present a relevant benefit or reason to pay attention.
-   Reflect the positioning and central marketing message.
-   Use language and a call to action appropriate to the audience and
    medium.
-   Be consistent with the pricing and packaging strategy, where these
    are mentioned or implied.

The visual treatment should support the message, not compensate for a
weak one.

## 5. Use of AI

The use of AI tools is permitted.

However, using AI does not remove your team's responsibility for the
quality and accuracy of the work. You are expected to review, edit,
question, and improve AI-generated material.

AI-generated content that is generic, inconsistent, poorly reasoned, or
disconnected from the customer and the course concepts will be assessed
accordingly. The quality of the final work matters more than the tool
used to produce it.

## 6. What to prioritise

**Prioritise content and context over user-interface design.**

You are not being asked to build a technically sophisticated website. A
simple, usable website with strong marketing content is preferable to a
visually impressive website with weak or incomplete content.

The assessment will focus on:

-   The effectiveness and relevance of the message.
-   The quality of the language and communication.
-   The consistency of the deliverables.
-   The reasoning behind the choices.
-   The suitability and effectiveness of the tactics.
-   The application of the concepts taught in the course.

Aesthetics and visual execution may contribute positively when they
strengthen an already sound deliverable. They will not make up for weak
strategy, unclear messaging, poor language, or missing links. A
beautiful presentation of a poor idea will remain a poor submission.

## 7. Assessment rubric

The following rubric describes the main areas that will guide
evaluation. The marks shown provide an indication of the relative
importance of each area.

| Assessment area | Marks | What will be considered |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer understanding and strategic alignment | 20 | Clarity of the target segment; meaningful use of the customer persona; understanding of customer needs and motivations; relevance of the proposed approach to the intended audience. |
| Positioning and central marketing message | 15 | Clarity and strength of positioning; relevance of the value proposition; presence of one coherent underlying marketing message across the deliverables. |
| Website and customer experience | 15 | Clarity and usefulness of the website content; logical information flow; alignment with the customer journey; suitable calls to action; clear and working access to every deliverable. |
| Propaganda strategy | 15 | Clarity of the intended influence or perception change; strength of the reasoning; suitability of tactics and channels; connection to the audience, positioning, and marketing objective. |
| Three LinkedIn posts | 15 | Relevance to the audience and platform; quality of language and message; distinct purpose for each post; consistency with the central message; suitability of calls to action. |
| Advertisement | 10 | Clarity and impact of the message; relevance to the intended audience and medium; strength of the benefit communicated; consistency with positioning and the overall marketing message. |
| Pricing, packaging, acquisition, and engagement integration | 10 | Appropriate use of the pricing and packaging decisions; a credible path to customer acquisition; consideration of how the customer relationship will be maintained and developed. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### How the rubric will be applied

The rubric is a guide to the areas and priorities that will inform
evaluation. It is not intended to function as a rigid checklist or an
absolute marking formula. The quality of the submission will be
considered as a whole, including how well the different components work
together.

Marks within each area will depend on the strength of the work, not
merely on whether a feature has been included. For example, mentioning a
customer persona is not the same as using it meaningfully to shape the
message. Similarly, including a call to action is not enough if the
action is unsuitable or disconnected from the customer's journey.

A submission may therefore perform differently across the areas, even
when all the requested artefacts are present.
